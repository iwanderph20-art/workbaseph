Frequently asked questions

How much does a Filipino video editor cost?

In 2026, an entry-level editor doing basic cuts, captions, and template-based edits runs $400–$600/month full-time. A mid-level editor comfortable with YouTube-style pacing, b-roll, and simple motion graphics costs $600–$1,000, a senior specialist handling advanced motion graphics and multi-platform delivery runs $1,000–$1,600, and a lead editor managing a small edit team costs $1,400–$2,200 — roughly 70–80% less than a US-based equivalent.

What software do Filipino video editors use?

Most are trained on Adobe Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve for long-form editing, CapCut for short-form and quick turnarounds, and After Effects for motion graphics and lower-thirds. Many also handle basic color correction, audio cleanup, and thumbnail design in Photoshop or Canva.

Can a Filipino editor keep up with a daily or weekly upload schedule?

Yes — a large share of Filipino editors work specifically for YouTube channels and agencies with recurring upload schedules, so they're used to turnaround windows of 24–72 hours per video. Screen for this directly: ask for their typical turnaround time and how many concurrent clients or videos they handle per week.

How do I share raw footage securely with a remote editor?

Cloud-based project tools work best — Frame.io and LucidLink are built for large video files and let you leave timestamped feedback directly on the cut. Google Drive or Dropbox work fine for smaller channels. Avoid emailing raw footage; set folder-level permissions instead of sharing your full drive.
