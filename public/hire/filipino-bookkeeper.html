What a Filipino bookkeeper handles

  • Recording daily transactions and reconciling bank statements
  • Managing accounts payable and receivable
  • Monthly financial reports — P&L, balance sheet, and cash flow
  • Processing payroll with appropriate software access
  • Preparing BAS/GST summaries (AU) or sales-tax reports (US)
  • Categorizing expenses and managing receipts via Dext or Hubdoc
  • Liaising with your external accountant or CPA at tax time

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to hire a Filipino bookkeeper?

In 2026, a Filipino bookkeeper typically costs $400–$600/month entry-level, $600–$900 mid-level, and $900–$1,400 for a senior bookkeeper or CPA. A CPA with AU/US tax experience runs $1,200–$1,800 — roughly 70–85% less than a US-based bookkeeper, who averages $3,500–$5,500/month.

Do I need a CPA, or will a bookkeeper do?

For most small to medium businesses, a skilled bookkeeper handles the day-to-day work — transactions, reconciliation, reporting — while a local CPA handles tax returns and strategic advice. A Filipino bookkeeper at $600–$900/month does the bulk of the work, with your local CPA reviewing at tax time.

Which accounting software do Filipino bookkeepers know?

QuickBooks Online and Xero are the most common, along with MYOB (popular for the Australian market), FreshBooks, Wave, and enterprise tools like Sage and NetSuite. Many also use receipt tools such as Dext and Hubdoc. Name your required software in the job post and screen for it.
