How to Get Clients as a Filipino VA Without Paying Platform Fees (2026)

By WorkBase PH · June 2026 · 7 min read

If you've ever paid for a membership subscription just to apply for jobs — only to hear nothing back — you know how frustrating it is. Many Filipino VAs spend ₱500–₱2,000 a month on platform fees before landing a single client. That stops now.

This guide walks you through practical, proven ways to land international clients in 2026 without paying platform fees, buying coins, or giving away a percentage of every paycheck you earn.

The reality: The best clients — the ones who pay well, respect your time, and stay long-term — are usually not the ones you find through bidding wars on Upwork. They're found through referrals, direct outreach, and platforms that match you to them.

Why Most Filipino VAs Struggle to Get Clients

Before the strategies, it helps to understand what's actually going wrong for most people:

  • Generic profiles — "I am a hardworking and dedicated VA" describes everyone. Clients can't tell what makes you different.
  • No portfolio or proof — Saying you know Canva or QuickBooks without showing any work is easy to dismiss.
  • Applying to everything — Sending the same message to every job post signals that you haven't read the post at all.
  • Competing on price alone — If your only edge is "I'm cheap," you'll always be replaced by someone cheaper. This race has no finish line.
  • Wrong platforms — Paying for a subscription on a platform flooded with thousands of applicants isn't a strategy — it's a lottery.

Strategy 1: Build a Profile That Does the Selling For You

Before you reach out to anyone or apply anywhere, your profile needs to answer one question clearly: Why should I hire you specifically?

A strong VA profile includes:

  • A specific headline — Not "Virtual Assistant" but "E-Commerce VA | Shopify + Gorgias + Email Management"
  • A 60–90 second intro video — Show your face, speak clearly, mention your niche and your top 3 skills. This alone puts you ahead of 80% of applicants who skip the video.
  • Specific tools you've used — ClickUp, Notion, Xero, HubSpot, Canva, etc. Be specific.
  • Results, not just tasks — "Managed social media" is weak. "Grew a client's Instagram from 1,200 to 8,400 followers in 6 months" is a reason to hire you.
  • A verified internet speed — Run a Speedtest and include the screenshot. It removes a major concern for employers immediately.

Niche down. "General VA" is the hardest role to get hired for because everyone offers it. "VA specializing in Amazon FBA seller support" or "Social media VA for fitness coaches" is far easier to market — and commands higher rates.

Strategy 3: LinkedIn Direct Outreach

LinkedIn is where thousands of small business owners, e-commerce entrepreneurs, and startup founders post about needing help — without ever posting a formal job. This is one of the most underused strategies by Filipino VAs.

How to do it:

  1. Set up a complete LinkedIn profile with your VA niche in the headline
  2. Search for: "e-commerce founder," "Amazon seller," "online business owner," "startup CEO" — filter by US, Australia, UK, Canada
  3. Follow them and engage genuinely with their posts (comments, reactions) for 1–2 weeks
  4. Send a short connection message — not a pitch. Just introduce yourself.
  5. After connecting, send one short message: what you do, who you help, and an offer to chat

Sample message: "Hi [Name], I noticed you're running [type of business]. I'm a Filipino VA specializing in [your niche] — I help business owners like you with [specific task]. Would you be open to a quick 15-minute call to see if I can help?"

This works because you're reaching someone before they post a job — when there's zero competition.

Strategy 4: Ask Every Client for a Referral

The best source of new clients is your existing clients. A referral from a happy client is worth 10 cold applications. Yet most VAs never ask.

After 3–4 months with a client, when the relationship is going well, simply say:

"I really enjoy working with you and I'm looking to take on one more client. If you know anyone who might need similar help, I'd really appreciate a referral."

Most people are happy to refer someone they trust. And referred clients almost always pay better and stay longer.

Strategy 5: Build a Simple Portfolio Page

You don't need a fancy website. A free Google Site, Notion page, or even a well-organized Google Drive folder shared as a link can serve as your portfolio.

Include:

  • A short bio and your niche
  • 3–5 samples of work (anonymized if needed)
  • Testimonials or kind words from past clients (screenshots are fine)
  • Your contact email or a Calendly link to book a call

When you apply for jobs or do outreach, your portfolio link is your proof. It immediately separates you from the majority of applicants who have nothing to show.

Strategy 6: Facebook Groups — The Right Way

Filipino VA Facebook groups are full of people selling to each other. Instead, find groups where your clients hang out:

  • Amazon FBA seller groups
  • E-commerce entrepreneur groups
  • Small business owner communities (US/AU/UK)
  • Real estate investor groups (great for transaction coordinator VAs)
  • Podcast host communities (great for show note writers, editors)

Join these groups, add genuine value in the comments for 2–3 weeks, then post once about what you do. "Selling" in these groups directly rarely works — but being helpful does. When someone posts "I need help with X," you're already known and trusted.

Strategy 7: Optimize for Being Found, Not Just Applying

The most scalable client acquisition strategy is one where clients find you. That means:

  • Complete your WorkBase PH profile fully — A 100% complete profile with video, speed test, and skills gets matched to employers automatically
  • Use keywords in your LinkedIn headline and summary — "Xero bookkeeper for e-commerce" or "Shopify VA for US brands" helps you show up in searches
  • Post on LinkedIn 2–3x per week — Share tips, lessons, or behind-the-scenes of your work. Consistency builds visibility over 2–3 months.

The Client Acquisition Checklist

  • Complete and niched-down profile with intro video
  • Signed up on WorkBase PH (free — get matched to employers)
  • Portfolio page with 3–5 work samples
  • LinkedIn profile optimized with niche keywords
  • Joined 2–3 client-side Facebook groups
  • Asked at least 1 past client for a referral
  • Sent 5 LinkedIn connection requests this week

Consistency beats intensity. Doing 3 outreach messages a day for 30 days beats sending 90 messages in one day and giving up. Client acquisition is a habit, not an event.
