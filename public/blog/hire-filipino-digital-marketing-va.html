How to Hire a Filipino Digital Marketing VA (2026)

By WorkBase PH · Updated September 2026 · 8 min read

"Digital marketing VA" covers a wider range of work than most job posts admit — SEO, paid advertising, email campaigns, and lead generation are genuinely different skill sets, even though the same person sometimes handles two or three of them. Hiring well starts with knowing which one you actually need.

This guide breaks down the channels, typical rates, and a screening process that separates real marketers from people who've read a few blog posts about it.

The Channels, Explained

  • SEO — keyword research, on-page optimization, content briefs, technical audits, and link building aimed at organic search rankings.
  • Paid advertising — Meta Ads, Google Ads, or TikTok Ads campaign setup, budget management, and optimization.
  • Email marketing — list segmentation, campaign and flow design in tools like Mailchimp or Klaviyo, and deliverability management.
  • Lead generation — prospecting, outbound outreach (email/LinkedIn), and appointment setting to fill a sales pipeline.

Some marketing VAs are true generalists who can execute across all four at a foundational level; specialists go deeper in one. Decide which you need before writing the job post.

What a Filipino Digital Marketing VA Typically Handles

  • Keyword research, content briefs, and on-page SEO fixes
  • Ad campaign setup, audience targeting, and budget optimization
  • Email list management, campaign design, and performance tracking
  • Landing page copy and conversion-rate testing suggestions
  • Competitor and market research
  • Monthly performance reporting tied to your actual goals (traffic, leads, sales)

How to Screen a Filipino Digital Marketing VA

Step 1: Name the channel and the goal in your job post

"Digital marketing VA needed" attracts generalist applicants of wildly varying quality. "SEO specialist to grow organic traffic for a Shopify store, must show past ranking results" attracts people who can actually prove it.

Step 2: Ask for evidence, not claims

Request screenshots of Google Search Console or Analytics data (with client names redacted if needed), ad account reporting dashboards, or email campaign performance stats. A results claim without a baseline, timeframe, or visible data isn't verifiable — ask for the underlying numbers.

Step 3: Give a small paid test

For SEO: ask for a one-page content brief or a mini audit of your homepage. For paid ads: ask them to sketch a campaign structure for a hypothetical budget. For email: ask for a sample welcome sequence outline. A short, paid exercise on your actual business reveals more than a portfolio of other people's wins.

Step 4: Check their reporting habits

Ask what a monthly report from them looks like and ask to see a redacted sample. Marketers who report clearly and honestly — including when something didn't work — are far more valuable long-term than ones who only report wins.

Onboarding for Faster Results

  • Share access properly. Add them as a user on Google Analytics, Search Console, ad accounts, and email platforms with appropriate permission levels — don't share login credentials directly.
  • Set one or two goals, not ten. A marketing hire spread across every possible metric rarely moves any of them. Agree on the one or two numbers that matter most for the first quarter.
  • Give brand and audience context up front. Ideal customer profile, brand voice, and past campaign learnings save weeks of trial and error.
  • Set a review cadence. Weekly check-ins for the first month, then monthly reporting once a rhythm is established.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Hiring a generalist for a specialist job. A generalist can execute across channels but rarely goes deep enough on any one to compete with a channel specialist.
  • Skipping data verification. Impressive-sounding results without underlying numbers are unverifiable and often overstated.
  • No clear goal. Marketing without a defined success metric drifts into busywork regardless of who's doing it.
  • Sharing account passwords instead of proper access. Always use role-based user access on ad and analytics platforms.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Filipino digital marketing VA cost?

In 2026, generalist marketing VAs handling execution tasks run $400-$800/month, mid-level specialists (SEO, email, or paid ads) run $800-$1,400, and senior strategists managing budgets and multiple channels run $1,400-$2,500.

What is the difference between a digital marketing VA and a social media manager?

A social media manager focuses specifically on organic content and community management across social platforms. A digital marketing VA has a broader remit that can include SEO, paid advertising, email marketing, and lead generation — channels beyond social. Some people do both; clarify scope before hiring so you know which you're getting.

Can a Filipino marketing VA run my paid ad campaigns?

Many can, particularly for Meta and Google Ads. Ask for past campaign screenshots or reporting dashboards (with results, not just spend) and confirm they've managed a budget similar in size to yours, since strategy shifts meaningfully between small and large ad budgets.

How do I know if a marketing VA's results claims are real?

Ask for read-only access to a past client's ad account or analytics dashboard if they're able to share it, or for a screen-recorded walkthrough of their reporting process. Vague claims like "grew traffic by 200%" without a baseline or timeframe are a red flag — a credible marketer can show their work, not just the outcome.

What tools should I expect a Filipino digital marketing VA to know?

Common tools include Google Analytics and Search Console for SEO, Meta Ads Manager and Google Ads for paid campaigns, Mailchimp or Klaviyo for email, and Semrush or Ahrefs for research. Expect familiarity with the tools relevant to the specific channel you're hiring for, not all of them at expert level.
