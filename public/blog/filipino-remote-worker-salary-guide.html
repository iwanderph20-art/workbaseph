Filipino Remote Worker Salary Guide 2026: What to Pay (By Role)

By WorkBase PH · May 2026 · 6 min read

One of the most common questions employers ask before hiring from the Philippines is: "What should I actually be paying?" Pay too little and you'll lose good candidates to better-paying employers. Pay what the market expects and you'll attract talent that stays.

This guide covers real 2026 salary benchmarks for the most common remote roles hired from the Philippines, along with a cost comparison that shows exactly how much you save versus hiring locally.

Note on currency: All rates below are in USD per month for full-time (40 hrs/week) work unless stated otherwise. Part-time rates are roughly 50–60% of the full-time equivalent, not 50%, because part-time roles often command a slight premium per hour.

What's Not Included (and What Is)

When you hire a Filipino remote worker as an independent contractor (the most common arrangement), your cost is generally the agreed salary — nothing more. Unlike local hires, you are typically not responsible for:

  • Employer payroll taxes or social security contributions
  • Health insurance premiums
  • Office space or equipment (most Filipino workers have their own setup)
  • Paid leave entitlements (though paying a 13th-month bonus is the norm and builds strong loyalty)

Filipino workers are responsible for their own tax filings with the BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) as independent contractors. You should use a simple service contract to formalise the engagement.

The 13th Month Bonus

The 13th month bonus is a deeply embedded norm in Philippine work culture. It's legally required for regular employees under Philippine law, and while it doesn't legally apply to independent contractors working for foreign employers, most Filipino remote workers expect it as a sign of respect and loyalty.

It's simply one extra month's salary paid in December (or split across mid-year and December). For a $700/month worker, that's an additional $700/year — a small cost that dramatically increases retention and loyalty.

Should You Pay in USD or PHP?

Most Filipino remote workers prefer to be paid in USD because it protects them from peso fluctuations and is easier to receive via Wise, Payoneer, or PayPal. USD rates are also the market standard on international hiring platforms.

You can agree on either, but quoting in USD simplifies negotiation and avoids awkward conversations when the exchange rate shifts.

The Bottom Line on Pay

Don't try to pay the absolute minimum. The Filipino remote worker market is competitive, and the best candidates have options. A mid-level VA earning $500/month receives dozens of job offers. If you're offering $350 for someone with 3+ years of experience, you'll attract junior candidates or lose your hire quickly.

Pay the mid-range for the role, add a 13th-month bonus, pay on time every time, and you'll have a loyal, productive team member who isn't quietly interviewing elsewhere.
