How to Hire Filipino IT Support Staff (2026)

By WorkBase PH · Updated September 2026 · 7 min read

IT support is one of the most established remote-hiring categories in the Philippines — the country's BPO industry built decades of experience staffing helpdesks and technical support lines for global companies. For a growing business, that means access to trained, English-fluent IT staff at a fraction of the cost of a local hire, with the added benefit of overnight coverage without overnight-shift pay.

This guide covers the support tiers, typical rates, and how to screen and securely onboard a remote IT hire.

Understanding Support Tiers

  • Tier 1 (helpdesk): first point of contact — password resets, basic software issues, ticket triage, and escalation of anything beyond scripted fixes.
  • Tier 2 (technical support): resolves deeper issues independently — network troubleshooting, software configuration, root-cause diagnosis.
  • Tier 3 / sysadmin: manages infrastructure, servers, and complex issues that Tier 2 escalates; often overlaps with a systems administrator role.

Most small businesses only need Tier 1 or Tier 2 — reserve Tier 3 / sysadmin hiring for when you're managing real infrastructure (servers, internal networks) rather than just supporting end users.

What Filipino IT Support Staff Typically Handle

  • Helpdesk ticket management and end-user troubleshooting
  • Software installation, updates, and license management
  • Basic network troubleshooting (Wi-Fi, VPN, connectivity)
  • Device setup and offboarding for new and departing employees
  • Documentation of common issues and internal knowledge-base upkeep
  • At the Tier 2+/sysadmin level: server maintenance, backups, and security patching

How to Screen a Filipino IT Support Hire

Step 1: Define the tier clearly in your job post

State the systems they'll support (Windows, macOS, specific software), the ticket volume, and whether the role is helpdesk-only or includes deeper troubleshooting. Vague postings attract a wide skill-level range that's hard to filter.

Step 2: Check certifications, but verify with a test

CompTIA A+ and Network+ are common baseline credentials. For specialized environments, look for vendor certifications matching your actual stack (Microsoft, Cisco, AWS, Google Cloud). A certification shows they studied the material — a practical test shows they can apply it.

Step 3: Give a scenario-based test

Describe a real support scenario you've actually encountered — a specific error message, a connectivity issue, a locked-out user — and ask them to walk through their troubleshooting steps. Their process (how they isolate the problem) matters more than whether they land on the exact right answer immediately.

Step 4: Confirm shift coverage and reliability

If you need coverage during a specific window (e.g., US business hours), confirm this upfront and ask about their internet/power backup plan — a Speedtest screenshot and a quick question about outage contingencies takes a minute and prevents unpleasant surprises during an actual incident.

Onboarding an IT Support Hire Securely

  • Least-privilege access. Grant only the systems and permission levels the role actually requires, not blanket admin access by default.
  • Password manager for shared credentials. Never send passwords over chat or email — use a tool like 1Password or LastPass with role-based sharing.
  • Mandatory two-factor authentication on every system they touch.
  • VPN or remote-access tooling with session logging so you have visibility into what was accessed and when.
  • Document your environment. A basic runbook of your systems, common issues, and escalation contacts dramatically shortens ramp-up time.

Access review: set a recurring reminder (quarterly is common) to review who has access to what, and revoke anything no longer needed. This applies to every remote hire with system access, not just IT staff.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Granting full admin access on day one. Start narrow and expand access as trust and tenure build.
  • Sharing credentials over chat or email. Always use a password manager with proper access controls.
  • Hiring Tier 2/3 skills for a Tier 1 problem set. Match the hire to your actual ticket complexity — overhiring wastes budget, underhiring creates bottlenecks.
  • No documentation. An undocumented environment turns every new hire into a slow, frustrating ramp-up regardless of their skill level.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does Filipino IT support staff cost?

In 2026, Tier 1 helpdesk support typically runs $500-$800/month full-time, Tier 2 technical support with troubleshooting depth runs $800-$1,300, and system administrators or IT managers run $1,300-$2,400.

What is the difference between Tier 1 and Tier 2 IT support?

Tier 1 support handles common, scripted issues — password resets, basic software troubleshooting, ticket triage — and escalates anything complex. Tier 2 support resolves deeper technical issues independently, such as network configuration, server problems, and root-cause diagnosis. Define which level you need based on how complex your typical support tickets actually are.

Is it safe to give a remote IT support hire access to company systems?

Yes, with the same access discipline you'd use for any remote hire: least-privilege access (only what the role requires), a password manager for shared credentials, mandatory two-factor authentication, and a VPN or remote-access tool with session logging. Expand access gradually as trust is established, and review access levels periodically.

Can a Filipino IT support hire cover after-hours or overnight shifts?

Yes — the time zone difference between the Philippines and the US actually works in your favor here, since Philippine daytime hours cover US overnight hours. This makes Filipino IT staff a common choice for extending support coverage without paying local overnight-shift premiums.

What certifications should I look for in a Filipino IT support hire?

CompTIA A+ or Network+ are common baseline certifications for helpdesk and Tier 1/2 roles. For more advanced roles, look for vendor-specific certifications relevant to your stack — Microsoft, Cisco, AWS, or Google Cloud, depending on what you actually run. A certification alone isn't proof of practical skill, so pair it with a hands-on test.
