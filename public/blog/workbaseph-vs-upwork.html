WorkBase PH vs Upwork — Which Is Better for Hiring Filipino Talent? (2026)

By WorkBase PH · Updated August 2026 · 11 min read

If you want to hire a Filipino remote worker, Upwork is probably the first platform that comes to mind — it's the biggest freelance marketplace in the world. But "biggest" and "best for your situation" aren't the same thing. WorkBase PH takes a very different approach, and for hiring Filipino talent specifically, the differences matter.

This is an honest breakdown of how the two compare on fees, talent, and the kind of hiring each is built for.

A note on Upwork's fees: Upwork changes its fee structure fairly often. The figures below reflect its published model as of 2026 — always check Upwork's current rates before making a decision.

Fees: Where the Money Actually Goes

Upwork

Upwork makes money on both sides of every transaction. Freelancers pay a service fee on what they earn (a flat 10% as of 2026) and spend paid "Connects" tokens to submit most proposals. Clients pay a marketplace fee plus a payment-processing charge on every payment they make. None of this is hidden — but it's charged continuously, so the more you pay your worker and the longer the engagement runs, the more the platform takes.

WorkBase PH

WorkBase PH charges one flat $29 to post a job. That's the whole employer cost — there's no marketplace fee, no per-payment cut, and no commission on the wages you pay. Specialists apply for free, don't buy tokens to be seen, and keep 100% of what you pay them. Because payment happens directly between you and your worker, the platform never sits between you and the money.

Bottom line on fees: For a long-term hire, WorkBase PH is almost always cheaper. Upwork's fees scale with what you pay the worker; WorkBase PH's $29 is the same whether that worker earns $500 or $5,000 a month. Upwork's fee model makes more sense for short, defined projects where you value escrow more than you mind the cut.

Talent: A Global Pool vs a Filipino One

Upwork

Upwork's talent pool is enormous and global — freelancers from every country and category, from developers to designers to virtual assistants. That breadth is a genuine strength if you don't care where your worker is based. The flip side: hiring is a bidding process. You post a job and freelancers send proposals, so you spend time filtering a wide, unfiltered field, and strong candidates are often juggling many clients at once.

WorkBase PH

WorkBase PH is built for one thing: hiring Filipino remote talent. Most specialists record a video and audio introduction, complete an internet speed test, and take a work-style assessment — so you can judge English fluency, communication style, and home-office setup before you ever message them. When you're hiring across timezones and can't meet in person, that context does a lot of the screening work a proposal simply can't.

How You Hire: Proposals vs Matching

Upwork

On Upwork you either post a job and wait for proposals, or search profiles and invite freelancers to apply. It works, but a popular job post can attract dozens of bids of wildly varying quality, and sorting them takes time. Reviews and the Job Success Score help — though newer freelancers with real skill can be hard to evaluate because they haven't built a track record yet.

WorkBase PH

WorkBase PH uses AI matching to surface the specialists whose skills and experience fit your job post, so you review a short list of relevant people rather than a pile of proposals. Combined with video intros, most employers move from posting to a shortlist in under 24 hours — without paying to unlock messages or contact details.

Payment Protection: Upwork's Real Edge

To be fair, there's one area where Upwork is genuinely stronger: payment infrastructure. Its escrow for fixed-price milestones and hourly work protection with time tracking give you recourse on a project with someone you've never worked with. If you're commissioning a single, well-defined deliverable from a stranger, that protection has real value.

WorkBase PH is built for direct, ongoing hires rather than escrowed one-off gigs. You pay your worker directly through your own method — which is exactly why there's no commission — but it also means you handle payment yourself instead of routing it through platform-held escrow. For a long-term team member you'll pay every month, most employers prefer that; for a one-time project, Upwork's escrow may be worth its fee.

Which Platform Should You Choose?

Choose WorkBase PH if: You specifically want to hire Filipino talent, you're building an ongoing role or team rather than commissioning a one-off project, and you'd rather pay a flat fee than a commission on every payment. Especially good if you want to see and hear candidates before messaging and keep your worker's full pay in their pocket.

Choose Upwork if: You're hiring for a short, defined project, you want escrow and payment protection with someone you've never worked with, or you need talent from anywhere in the world rather than the Philippines specifically.

The Honest Truth

Upwork is a powerful, well-built marketplace — for the global, project-based freelancing it was designed around. If you want escrow, worldwide reach, and a one-off deliverable, it's hard to beat.

But if your goal is a long-term Filipino hire — a VA, a bookkeeper, a designer, a customer-support rep who's part of your team month after month — the economics and the screening both tilt toward WorkBase PH. A flat fee instead of an ongoing commission, video intros instead of proposals, and a pool built specifically for Filipino remote work.

Neither platform is objectively "better." They're built for different jobs. Match the platform to the kind of hire you're making.
