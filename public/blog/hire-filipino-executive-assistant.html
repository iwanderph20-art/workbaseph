How to Hire a Filipino Executive Assistant (2026)

By WorkBase PH · Updated July 2026 · 10 min read

An executive assistant is the highest-leverage remote hire a busy founder or executive can make. A great EA doesn't just clear tasks — they protect your time, guard your inbox, run your calendar with judgment, and quietly keep your world moving so you can focus on the work only you can do. The Philippines produces exceptional EAs: detail-oriented, discreet, fluent in Western business norms, and available for a fraction of the cost of a local hire.

This guide explains how an EA differs from a general VA, what a Filipino executive assistant costs in 2026, how to screen for the judgment and discretion the role demands, and how to onboard them into a true right hand.

Executive Assistant vs General VA: Know What You're Hiring

These are not the same role, and hiring one when you need the other is the most common mistake. A general VA executes defined tasks: "book this flight," "enter these leads," "format this doc." An executive assistant operates a level up — they own outcomes, not just tasks.

  • A VA waits for instructions. An EA anticipates. They notice the gap in your week, the follow-up you forgot, the conflict on your calendar — and handle it before you ask.
  • A VA completes. An EA prioritizes. They decide what deserves your attention and what they can absorb or deflect.
  • A VA is given access. An EA is trusted with judgment. They speak on your behalf, manage sensitive information, and represent you to clients and your team.

Because you're buying judgment and discretion, an EA costs more than a task VA and must be screened differently — for how they think, not just what they can do.

  • Calendar management — scheduling, rescheduling, resolving conflicts, protecting focus time
  • Inbox management — triage, drafting replies in your voice, flagging what truly needs you
  • Meeting coordination — agendas, notes, action items, and follow-up

  • Light project management — keeping initiatives on track across tools like Notion, Asana, or ClickUp
  • Personal admin — appointments, reminders, and errands that free your headspace

How to Screen a Filipino Executive Assistant

You cannot screen an EA the way you screen a task VA. A polished resume tells you little about judgment. Test how they think under realistic conditions.

Step 1: Write a job post that signals the level

Describe the reality of the role — "manage a founder's calendar and inbox, coordinate with clients, anticipate needs, handle confidential information" — plus the tools, hours, and rate. Serious EAs self-select toward posts that respect the seniority of the work and skip vague "VA needed" listings.

Step 2: Use situational and judgment questions

Interviews should probe decision-making, not task lists. Strong prompts include:

  • "My inbox has 200 unread emails after a week off. Walk me through exactly how you'd triage it."
  • "Two important meetings just double-booked. What do you do?"
  • "A client emails something upset while I'm unreachable for six hours. How do you handle it?"

You're listening for a clear method, sensible prioritization, and smart clarifying questions — the fingerprints of real EA experience.

Step 3: Give a short paid scenario task

Set up a sample messy inbox and calendar and ask them to reorganize it and explain their choices, or to draft three replies of varying sensitivity. A 30–45 minute paid exercise shows judgment and communication far better than any claim. Pay for it — experienced EAs expect that.

Step 4: Assess communication and discretion

  • Writing: Their emails represent you. Look for clarity, warmth, and correct, professional tone.
  • Discretion: Ask how they've handled confidential information before. The right answer is careful and non-specific — an EA who overshares a past employer's secrets will overshare yours.
  • Setup: Request a Speedtest screenshot and confirm a quiet workspace, since real-time calendar and call support needs reliability.

The trust test: The best EAs make you feel calmer within the first two weeks — things get handled without you noticing the effort. If you find yourself checking their work constantly after a month, either the fit or the onboarding is off. A true EA earns the right to be left alone.

Onboarding Your EA Into a True Right Hand

An EA needs deep context to exercise judgment on your behalf. The first month is an investment that pays back for years.

  • Week 1 — Context and access: Share how you work, your priorities, your key relationships, and your pet peeves. Set up delegated access to calendar and inbox (not shared passwords), and tool access with two-factor authentication.
  • Week 1–2 — Shadow and review: Let them see how you currently handle your inbox and schedule. Review their drafts and decisions before they go out so you can calibrate their judgment to yours.
  • Week 2–4 — Widening autonomy: As their calls match what you'd have done, hand over more. Move from "ask me first" to "handle it and tell me."
  • Ongoing — A weekly sync: A short weekly check-in on priorities, what's coming, and what they need keeps you aligned without micromanaging.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Hiring a task VA for an EA role. If you need anticipation and judgment, screen and pay for it. A cheap task VA in an EA seat frustrates everyone.
  • Skipping the judgment test. Resumes don't reveal decision-making. Always run situational questions and a paid scenario.
  • Under-investing in onboarding. An EA can't anticipate your needs without deep context. Front-load the first month.
  • Sharing passwords instead of delegating. Use proper delegated access and 2FA — it's safer and more professional.
  • Never letting go. If you refuse to hand over real authority, you've hired an expensive typist, not an EA. Trust, verify, then trust more.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an executive assistant and a virtual assistant?

A general VA executes defined tasks you assign. An executive assistant operates one level up: they manage your calendar and inbox with judgment, prioritize on your behalf, coordinate with clients and your team, and anticipate what you need before you ask. You're hiring for judgment and discretion, not just task completion — which is why an EA costs more and is screened differently.

How much does a Filipino executive assistant cost?

In 2026, a capable Filipino EA typically costs $600–$1,000/month for solid mid-level support, and $1,000–$1,600 for a senior EA with years of experience supporting founders or executives. That's roughly 70–80% less than a US-based EA, who commonly costs $5,000–$7,000/month plus benefits.

Can a Filipino EA work in my time zone?

Yes. Many Filipino EAs are accustomed to working US, UK, or Australian hours so they can manage your calendar and inbox in real time. Because calendar and inbox work benefits from live overlap with you, agree on the core hours you need covered and confirm the candidate can sustain that schedule long-term, not just at the start.

Is it safe to give an executive assistant access to my email and calendar?

Yes, with standard precautions. Use delegated access (Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 both support inbox and calendar delegation) rather than sharing your password, enable two-factor authentication, and include a confidentiality clause in the contract. Start with limited scope, review their handling for the first couple of weeks, and expand access as trust builds.

How do I know if a candidate has real executive assistant experience?

Ask situational questions: how they'd triage an overloaded inbox, resolve a double-booked calendar, or handle a sensitive message to a client. A real EA answers with a clear method and asks smart clarifying questions. Pair that with a short paid scenario task — such as reorganizing a messy sample inbox and calendar — to see their judgment in action rather than just hearing claims.
