Cost of a Filipino VA vs. a US-Based Employee (2026)

By WorkBase PH · Updated September 2026 · 8 min read

"How much cheaper is it, really?" is usually the first question a first-time employer asks before hiring a Filipino virtual assistant — and it's a fair one. The honest answer requires looking past the sticker price on both sides, because a US salary and a Filipino VA's rate aren't measuring the same thing.

This guide breaks down the real, fully-loaded cost of each option, the hidden costs that don't show up in a job offer, and a simple way to calculate your actual savings before you post a role.

The "loaded" US figure includes salary plus the employer-side costs covered below — it's the number that actually leaves your bank account, not just the offer letter figure.

What Loads a US Employee's Real Cost Above Salary

A US W-2 hire's advertised salary is rarely the full cost. Typical additions include:

  • Employer payroll taxes — Social Security, Medicare, and unemployment insurance, generally 7-10% of salary.
  • Health insurance — employer contributions commonly run $6,000-$10,000+ per employee per year for a family plan.
  • Paid time off and holidays — you're paying for roughly 15-25 days a year the person isn't producing.
  • Workers' compensation and other insurance — required in most states, a smaller but real add-on.
  • Equipment, software licenses, and office space or home-office stipend.
  • Recruiting and onboarding cost — job board fees, interview time, and ramp-up time before the hire is fully productive.

Rule of thumb: multiply a US base salary by 1.25-1.4 to estimate the fully loaded annual cost. A $50,000 salary is closer to $62,500-$70,000 once these are added.

What You're Actually Paying For With a Filipino VA

Most employers engage a Filipino VA as an independent contractor rather than a payroll employee, which removes the employer-side tax and benefits obligations above — the rate you agree on is close to the full cost. That's the biggest structural reason for the gap, on top of the underlying cost-of-living difference between the two countries.

What doesn't change: you're still buying real skill and real hours. The Philippines has one of the largest English-fluent, college-educated remote workforces in the world, built over two decades of serving US, UK, and Australian companies through the BPO and freelance industries. The lower rate reflects local cost of living and currency, not lower ability.

A note on worker classification: contractor vs. employee rules vary by country and by how the working relationship is actually structured (hours, exclusivity, control over how work gets done). This guide isn't legal or tax advice — if you're unsure how your arrangement should be classified, check with your own accountant or employment attorney before you hire.

Does Cheaper Mean Lower Quality?

Not inherently — but it does mean screening matters more, not less, because the price signal you'd normally rely on ("expensive must mean good") doesn't work the same way across currencies. The employers who get burned are usually the ones who skip screening because the hire felt "cheap enough to not worry about."

  • Ask for a short paid test relevant to the actual role before committing to ongoing work.
  • Check English fluency on a live call, not just written messages.
  • Confirm time zone overlap and internet reliability (a Speedtest screenshot takes ten seconds to request).
  • Start with a trial period or smaller scope before handing over full responsibility.

How to Calculate Your Actual Savings

  1. Get the loaded US cost. Take the salary you'd realistically pay for the role locally and multiply by 1.25-1.4.
  2. Get the Filipino VA rate for the same scope. Rate guides for specific roles (bookkeeping, social media, executive assistance) are linked below — match the level of responsibility, not just the job title.
  3. Subtract and annualize. (Loaded US cost) minus (VA annual cost) = your annual savings for that one role.
  4. Decide what the savings fund. Many employers use the gap to hire two or three specialized roles instead of one generalist, or to reinvest the difference into growth (marketing, product, another hire).

Common Mistakes When Comparing Cost

  • Comparing hourly rate only. A lower hourly rate with unclear scope can end up costing more in mismanaged time than a higher rate with clear deliverables.
  • Ignoring the US-side loaded cost. Comparing a VA's full rate against a bare US salary (with no taxes or benefits added) understates your real savings.
  • Skipping screening because the hire is inexpensive. The cost of a bad hire — your time, redone work, missed deadlines — applies regardless of what you're paying.
  • Assuming one rate fits every role. A bookkeeper, a developer, and a social media strategist have very different rate bands — check the specific guide for the role you're hiring.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much cheaper is a Filipino VA than a US employee?

For comparable admin, support, or content work, a Filipino VA typically costs 60-80% less than a US-based employee once you count salary, payroll taxes, and benefits. A US admin hire running $45,000-$55,000/year loaded often lands a Filipino VA doing similar work in the $6,000-$12,000/year range.

What hidden costs does a US employee have that a Filipino VA doesn't?

A US W-2 employee typically adds employer payroll taxes, health insurance contributions, paid time off, workers' comp, equipment, office space, and recruiting costs on top of salary — often 25-40% above base pay. A Filipino VA engaged as an independent contractor has none of these employer-side obligations, though you should confirm your own tax and worker-classification requirements with an accountant.

Does a lower cost mean lower quality?

No — the cost gap reflects the cost of living and currency difference between the US and the Philippines, not skill level. The Philippines has a large English-fluent, college-educated workforce with decades of experience serving US, UK, and Australian clients through the BPO industry. Quality still varies by individual, so screening matters regardless of price.

Is a Filipino VA a contractor or an employee?

Most employers engage Filipino VAs as independent contractors rather than employees, which is why there's no payroll tax or benefits obligation on the employer side. Worker classification rules vary by country, so if you're unsure whether your arrangement should be a contractor or an employee, check with a local accountant or employment attorney.

How do I calculate my real savings before hiring?

Take the fully loaded cost of the US hire (salary plus payroll tax, benefits, and equipment, usually salary times 1.25-1.4), subtract the Filipino VA's expected monthly rate for the same scope of work, and multiply by 12. Most employers find the annual savings alone can fund several other roles or a meaningful marketing budget.
