How Much Does a Filipino Bookkeeper Cost? (2026 Salary Guide)

By WorkBase PH · May 2026 · 6 min read

Hiring a bookkeeper is one of the highest-leverage moves a small business can make — and hiring one from the Philippines is one of the most cost-effective ways to do it. Filipino bookkeepers are English-fluent, familiar with international accounting software like QuickBooks, Xero, and MYOB, and typically cost a fraction of what a local bookkeeper would charge in the US, Australia, or the UK.

This guide covers exactly what to expect to pay, what skill level each price range gets you, and how to hire a Filipino bookkeeper the right way.

What a Filipino Bookkeeper Typically Handles

Most Filipino bookkeepers working remotely for international clients are comfortable with the following:

  • Recording daily transactions (sales, expenses, receipts)
  • Bank and credit card reconciliation
  • Accounts payable and receivable management
  • Monthly financial statements (P&L, balance sheet, cash flow)
  • Payroll processing for small teams
  • QuickBooks Online, Xero, Wave, MYOB, FreshBooks
  • Invoice creation and follow-up
  • Expense categorization and reporting
  • GST/VAT recording (for AU/UK clients — they understand the basics)
  • Supporting tax preparation (they gather and organize; your local accountant files)

Important distinction: A Filipino remote bookkeeper organizes and records your financials. For tax filing, you'll still need a licensed accountant in your home country. Think of your Filipino bookkeeper as the person who keeps everything clean so your accountant's bill stays low.

How to Screen a Filipino Bookkeeper

Bookkeeping requires more careful screening than general VA roles because errors cost money. Here's a practical screening process:

Step 1: Check their software proficiency

Ask specifically which version of the software they've used and how long. "I know QuickBooks" is vague — "I've managed QuickBooks Online for 3 clients over 2 years, primarily for e-commerce businesses" is a real answer.

Step 2: Give a short practical test

Send a small sample task — a set of 10–15 transactions to categorize, or a mini bank reconciliation exercise. Even a 20-minute test separates experienced candidates from those who overstated their skills.

Step 3: Ask about error handling

Ask: "Tell me about a time you caught an error in the books — yours or a previous one. What happened?" Experienced bookkeepers have stories. They also have systems to prevent repeat mistakes.

Step 4: Check internet reliability

Cloud accounting software requires consistent internet. Ask for a Speedtest screenshot — you want at least 20 Mbps download and a stable connection. Power interruptions are a reality in some provinces; ask if they have a backup (UPS, mobile data).

Step 5: Verify availability for month-end

Month-end close is the crunch period. Confirm your candidate is available and understands that some weeks — especially end of month — may require more focused attention.

Red Flags to Watch For

  • Cannot name specific clients or industries they've worked in
  • Unfamiliar with your accounting software but claims they can "learn fast" — for bookkeeping, you want proven experience
  • Vague on what financial reports they've produced
  • No portfolio or sample reports to share (experienced bookkeepers often have anonymized samples)
  • Unwilling to do a short practical test

Is a Filipino Bookkeeper Right for Your Business?

A Filipino remote bookkeeper works well for:

  • Small businesses and startups with 1–50 employees
  • E-commerce stores (Shopify, Amazon FBA, WooCommerce)
  • Service businesses billing monthly retainers
  • Agencies managing client accounts
  • Entrepreneurs who need clean books but aren't ready for a full-time in-house hire

It may not be the right fit if:

  • You need someone to handle complex tax strategy (that's an accountant's job)
  • Your jurisdiction requires a locally licensed bookkeeper by law
  • You need real-time, in-office presence for cash handling or physical records
