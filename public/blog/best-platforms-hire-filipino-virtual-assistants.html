Which Platform Should You Use?

The right platform depends on three things:

  • How much time you have to screen candidates. If you're busy running a business, platforms that show you video intros and match (like WorkBase PH) save you hours per hire.
  • Whether this is long-term or project-based. For ongoing roles, direct hiring platforms beat Upwork on total cost. For short projects, Upwork's built-in protections are worth it.
  • Your budget. The cheapest platform per month isn't always cheapest per hire. A $60/month job board that takes 20 hours to find one good hire is more expensive than a $150/month platform that matches you in a day.

Our take: For most small to mid-size businesses hiring their first Filipino VA or building a remote team, WorkBase PH offers the best combination of candidate quality, speed, and fair pricing — for both the employer and the worker.

What to Look for in Any Filipino Hiring Platform

Before committing to any platform, run it through these five questions:

1. Can you see full candidate profiles before paying?

Some platforms show you a teaser profile — name, title, and a short summary — and lock contact details behind a subscription. This creates friction and can lead to paying for access to candidates who aren't actually available or relevant. The best platforms let you evaluate profile quality before committing.

2. Is there any vetting, or is it fully self-reported?

A platform with zero vetting means every profile is exactly as good as the candidate has presented themselves. Inflated skills, outdated availability, and inactive profiles are common on open job boards. Platforms that verify identity, check internet speed, or require a work sample submission give you a meaningful signal before you invest screening time.

3. Does the platform protect workers from scam employers?

This is underappreciated by employers but matters enormously for candidate quality. Platforms where fake job posts and non-payment are common problems attract desperate or inexperienced workers — not the professionals you want. A platform that verifies employers attracts better candidates because those candidates feel safer applying.

4. What's the total cost per hire — not just the monthly fee?

A $69/month platform where it takes 3 weeks and 20 hours of screening to find one hire costs more than a $150/month platform where you find a qualified candidate in 3 days. Factor in your time, not just the subscription price.

5. Is there a way to assess communication quality before an interview?

Communication is everything in remote work. Platforms that include video introductions, audio clips, or structured written responses to screening questions let you assess how a candidate communicates before you ever schedule a call. This alone can cut your interview time in half.

Platform-by-Platform: Deeper Breakdown

WorkBase PH — How the Matching Works

You're not browsing a database of 200,000 resumes. You're reviewing a curated shortlist of candidates who genuinely fit what you described.

The employer-side vetting matters too: only verified employers can post jobs, which means Filipino specialists on the platform aren't sifting through scam listings. This makes WorkBase PH one of the few platforms where the trust flows both ways.

OnlineJobs.ph — The Volume Play

OnlineJobs.ph launched in 2009 and became the dominant Filipino job board largely because it was first. Its core model hasn't changed much: employers pay a monthly subscription to browse profiles and post jobs, workers create profiles for free. The database is genuinely massive — hundreds of thousands of active and inactive profiles across every role category imaginable.

The challenge is the signal-to-noise ratio. Because anyone can create a profile and there's no vetting process, a search for "bookkeeper" returns everyone from fresh graduates to experienced CPAs — and distinguishing between them requires reading every profile carefully. Many experienced employers on OnlineJobs.ph have developed their own multi-step screening systems (custom application forms, skills tests, trial tasks) to compensate for the platform's lack of built-in filtering. That works if you have the time and experience to build it. If you don't, it's a steep learning curve.

VirtualStaff.ph — When You Want It Handled

VirtualStaff.ph is best understood as a hybrid between a staffing agency and a job board. You post your requirements, they help source candidates, and the platform handles employment contracts, payroll processing, and HR documentation under their system. This removes a significant administrative burden, particularly for businesses that need to stay compliant or are scaling a team quickly.

The tradeoff is cost and distance from your worker. When payroll and contracts go through an intermediary, the direct employer-worker relationship is more formal and slightly less personal. Some employers also find the service fees on top of worker salaries make VirtualStaff.ph significantly more expensive than self-managed hiring. For businesses that value process and compliance over cost efficiency, it's a good fit. For solo founders or small teams, the overhead often isn't worth it.

Upwork Philippines — Project Work Only

The math on Upwork for long-term hires doesn't work in your favour. Upwork charges employers a 5% service fee on payments. Workers pay between 10–20% on what they earn through Upwork (depending on their total billings with you). This means a Filipino worker who needs to net $700/month has to charge you roughly $780–$840/month to cover their Upwork fees — and you pay an additional 5% on top of that. The effective premium over direct hiring is 15–25%.

For a three-month project worth $3,000, that premium is manageable and the platform's built-in payment protection and work diary are worth it. For a two-year full-time engagement, you're paying $2,000–$5,000 more per year than you would through a direct hire platform. Most experienced remote employers move long-term contractors off Upwork to a direct arrangement — many platforms including WorkBase PH are designed to support exactly that kind of ongoing, direct relationship.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which platform has the most Filipino VAs?

OnlineJobs.ph has the largest absolute number of Filipino worker profiles — built over 15+ years. However, volume doesn't equal quality. WorkBase PH has a smaller but curated pool where every profile has a video and audio intro, which most employers find more useful when they want to hire efficiently rather than browse exhaustively.

Can I use multiple platforms at once?

Yes — many experienced employers post on two platforms simultaneously to maximize reach, then screen from both. The most common combination is WorkBase PH (for matched candidates with video intros) plus OnlineJobs.ph (for volume). Just be prepared to manage applications from two sources.

Are there free platforms to hire Filipino VAs?

Facebook groups (particularly "Filipino Virtual Assistants" and similar communities) allow free job posting and have active membership. The quality is highly variable and there's no vetting or payment protection, but for employers willing to do extensive screening themselves, it can work for lower-stakes roles. LinkedIn is also free to post on but less used by Filipino VA candidates than dedicated platforms.

How do I verify a candidate found on any platform?

Regardless of platform, your own screening matters. Ask for an internet speed test (Speedtest.net screenshot), conduct a short video call to assess communication, give a small paid skills test relevant to the role, and request one or two verifiable references from previous employers. For financial roles (bookkeeper, accounting VA), ask for a sample report from a previous engagement (anonymized). These steps catch misrepresentation that no platform can fully prevent.

What if I've had bad experiences hiring Filipino VAs before?

Almost always, bad hiring experiences trace back to one of three things: vague job descriptions that attracted the wrong candidates, inadequate screening before hiring, or poor onboarding after the hire. The platform is rarely the root cause. Switching platforms helps if the previous one had genuinely low-quality candidates — but improving your job post, your screening process, and your first-30-days onboarding will have a bigger impact than platform choice alone.
