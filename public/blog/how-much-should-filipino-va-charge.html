How Much Should a Filipino VA Charge in 2026? (Know Your Worth)

By WorkBase PH · June 2026 · 6 min read

One of the most common questions Filipino remote workers ask is: "Am I charging too little?" The honest answer — for most — is yes. Many Filipino VAs undercharge because they compare themselves to local Philippine salaries instead of international market rates.

This guide breaks down exactly what Filipino virtual assistants should be charging in 2026 — by role, by experience level, and by the value you actually deliver. Use this as your benchmark before your next client negotiation.

Remember: When you work for international clients, you're competing in a global market — not a local one. Your rates should reflect the value you deliver to a US, Australian, UK, or Canadian business, not what a local company might pay.

Why So Many Filipino VAs Undercharge

There are a few reasons this happens — and all of them are worth understanding so you can avoid the same trap:

1. Comparing to local salaries

The Philippine minimum wage is roughly ₱610/day in Metro Manila — around $11/day. When you're used to that context, charging $6/hour feels like a lot. But for your US client, $6/hour is extremely affordable. The comparison point should be what they'd pay locally — not what they'd pay in the Philippines.

2. Fear of losing the client

Many VAs accept lowball offers because they're afraid the client will say no. But clients who push you to $3/hour and make you feel replaceable are not the clients you want. Good employers understand that fair pay equals better retention.

3. Not accounting for all costs

Your internet bill, electricity, equipment depreciation, software subscriptions — these are business expenses. Your rate needs to cover them plus your actual income. A $4/hour rate after costs can quickly feel like ₱150/hour — far less than it sounds.

4. Not raising rates as skills improve

If you've been with a client for 12 months and learned new tools, taken on more responsibility, and consistently delivered — your rate should reflect that. Loyal clients rarely leave over a $1–$2/hour increase if you've proven your value.

How to Know If You're Being Underpaid Right Now

Ask yourself these questions:

  • Has your rate stayed the same for more than 12 months?
  • Are you doing more tasks now than when you started — for the same pay?
  • Did you learn new tools or skills on the job that your client now relies on?
  • Are new VAs on other platforms advertising the same skills for a higher rate?

If you answered yes to any of these — it's time to have the rate conversation.

Script for asking for a raise: "I've been with you for [X months] and in that time I've taken on [new responsibilities]. Based on current market rates for my skill set, I'd like to discuss adjusting my rate to [new rate]. I'm committed to continuing to deliver the same quality of work, and I want to make sure we're both on a sustainable arrangement."

What Justifies a Higher Rate

You can confidently charge on the higher end of any range if you can say yes to most of these:

  • You have 2+ years of experience in the specific role
  • You're proficient in tools the client needs (Xero, ClickUp, Shopify, etc.)
  • You have a portfolio, testimonials, or measurable results to show
  • You have reliable internet (25+ Mbps), backup power, and your own equipment
  • You're available during the client's working hours without being asked twice
  • You communicate proactively and don't wait to be chased for updates

Part-Time vs Full-Time Rates

If a client wants you part-time (10–20 hours/week), your hourly rate should be slightly higher than your full-time rate — not lower. Part-time arrangements mean less income security for you and often more context-switching. A fair premium for part-time is 10–20% above your standard hourly rate.
