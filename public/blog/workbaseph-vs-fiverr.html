WorkBase PH vs Fiverr — Which Is Better for Hiring Filipino Talent? (2026)

By WorkBase PH · Updated September 2026 · 10 min read

If you need something done fast and don't want to manage a hiring process, Fiverr is the platform most people reach for — buy a "gig," get a fixed deliverable. WorkBase PH solves a different problem: hiring an ongoing Filipino remote team member. For a one-off task the two aren't really competing, but for anything that turns into a recurring role, the differences add up fast.

This is an honest breakdown of how the two compare on fees, hiring model, and the kind of work each is built for.

A note on Fiverr's fees: Fiverr changes its fee structure fairly often. The figures below reflect its published model as of 2026 — always check Fiverr's current rates before making a decision.

Fees: Where the Money Actually Goes

Fiverr

Fiverr takes a flat 20% commission from every seller on every order, no matter how long they've worked with a buyer or how large the order is. Buyers pay an additional service fee on top of the order price. That 20% never tapers off — if you rebook the same freelancer every month for a year, Fiverr takes its cut every single time.

WorkBase PH

WorkBase PH charges one flat $29 to post a job. That's the whole employer cost — there's no per-order commission, no seller cut, and no fee on the wages you pay. Specialists apply for free and keep 100% of what you pay them. Because payment happens directly between you and your worker, the platform never sits between you and the money after the hire is made.

Bottom line on fees: For an ongoing hire, WorkBase PH wins easily — Fiverr's 20% commission is charged forever, while WorkBase PH's $29 is a one-time cost regardless of how long the role runs or how much you pay. For a single small task you need done once, Fiverr's fixed-price gig can still be the simpler pick.

Hiring Model: Gigs vs an Ongoing Team Member

Fiverr

Fiverr is built around gigs — sellers list fixed-scope packages (Basic/Standard/Premium tiers) at a set price, and buyers purchase directly with little back-and-forth. That's fast for a logo, a short video, or a batch of product descriptions. It's not designed for open-ended, ongoing work — there's no job posting, no interview process, and no structure for a role that evolves over months.

WorkBase PH

WorkBase PH is built for hiring an actual team member. You describe the role, get AI-matched to relevant Filipino candidates, review video and audio intros, and hire someone whose responsibilities can grow with your business — a VA, a support rep, a bookkeeper who works for you every month, not a one-time deliverable.

Talent: A Global Gig Catalog vs a Filipino Hiring Pool

Fiverr

Fiverr's catalog is enormous and global, covering nearly every skill imaginable. Sellers are ranked by level (New Seller through Top Rated) and reviews, which helps you spot proven gig-doers. But the platform isn't built around any one country's talent — you're browsing a worldwide catalog of packaged services, not screening candidates for a role.

WorkBase PH

WorkBase PH is built for one thing: hiring Filipino remote talent. Most specialists record a video and audio introduction, complete an internet speed test, and take a work-style assessment — so you can judge English fluency, communication style, and home-office setup before you ever message them. That context matters far more for an ongoing hire than a gig review score does.

Payment Protection: Fiverr's Real Edge

To be fair, there's one area where Fiverr is genuinely convenient: payment is built in. Fiverr holds the buyer's payment and releases it to the seller after delivery, giving buyers straightforward recourse on a single, well-defined order with someone they've never worked with before.

WorkBase PH is built for direct, ongoing hires rather than one-off gig purchases. You pay your worker directly through your own method — which is exactly why there's no commission — but it also means you handle payment yourself instead of routing it through platform-held funds. For a long-term team member you'll pay every month, most employers prefer that; for a single small deliverable, Fiverr's built-in payment flow may be worth its fee.

Which Platform Should You Choose?

Choose WorkBase PH if: You want to hire an ongoing Filipino team member — a VA, a bookkeeper, a support rep, a writer — rather than buy a one-time deliverable, and you'd rather pay a flat fee once than a 20% commission on every payment forever. Especially good if you want to see and hear candidates before messaging and keep your worker's full pay in their pocket.

Choose Fiverr if: You need a single, well-defined task done fast — a logo, a short video edit, a batch of product photos — with built-in payment protection and no interview process, and you don't need talent from the Philippines specifically.

The Honest Truth

Fiverr is genuinely good at what it's built for: fast, fixed-scope gigs from a global catalog, with payment handled for you. If you need a one-off deliverable and don't want to run a hiring process, it's hard to beat for speed.

But if your goal is a long-term Filipino hire — someone who works for you every month, not just once — the economics tilt heavily toward WorkBase PH. A one-time $29 instead of a 20% cut on every payment forever, video intros instead of a gig catalog, and a pool built specifically for Filipino remote work.

Neither platform is objectively "better." They're built for different jobs. Match the platform to the kind of hire you're making.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Fiverr take a commission from freelancers?

Yes. Fiverr charges sellers a flat 20% commission on every order, deducted automatically before payout. Buyers also pay a separate service fee on top of the order price. WorkBase PH takes no commission at all — specialists keep 100% of the wages you pay them, and employers pay only a flat $29 to post a job. Fiverr updates its fee structure periodically, so check its current rates before comparing.

Is Fiverr good for hiring Filipino workers specifically?

Fiverr has Filipino sellers across many gig categories, but it's a global gig marketplace covering every skill and country, so you're browsing an enormous, unfiltered catalog rather than a pool built for Filipino remote hiring. WorkBase PH is built specifically for hiring Filipino remote talent, with candidates who record video and audio intros, complete an internet speed test, and take a work-style assessment — context that matters when you're hiring across timezones and can't meet in person.

Can I hire someone on Fiverr for an ongoing, full-time role?

Fiverr is built around gigs — fixed-scope packages a seller lists at a set price, meant for discrete, well-defined deliverables. You can rebook the same seller repeatedly, but there's no ongoing employment structure, and the 20% seller commission applies to every single order, so costs scale continuously. WorkBase PH is built for direct, ongoing hires: post once for $29 and pay your worker directly for however long the role runs, with no per-transaction cut.

Which is cheaper, WorkBase PH or Fiverr?

For an ongoing hire, WorkBase PH is cheaper by a wide margin. Fiverr's 20% seller commission plus the buyer service fee are charged on every order, so a worker you pay monthly loses a fifth of their earnings indefinitely. WorkBase PH charges one flat $29 to post a job and takes nothing from wages, so a long-term hire costs the same $29 whether you pay them $500 or $5,000 a month. For a single small task, Fiverr's fixed-price gig can still be the faster, simpler choice.

Does Fiverr hold payment until the work is delivered?

Yes — Fiverr holds the buyer's payment and releases it to the seller after delivery is marked complete (or automatically after a review window), which gives buyers some protection on a one-off order. WorkBase PH is built for direct, ongoing hires: you pay your worker directly through your own method, which is why there's no commission, but it means you manage payment yourself rather than through platform-held funds.
