How to Hire a Filipino Shopify Virtual Assistant (2026)

By WorkBase PH · Updated July 2026 · 9 min read

Running a Shopify store is a hundred small jobs a day — uploading products, fixing listings, answering "where's my order?", installing apps, cleaning up inventory. It's exactly the kind of work that eats an owner's time and rarely needs an owner to do it. A Filipino Shopify virtual assistant can take the whole operational layer off your plate, and the Philippines has a deep bench of e-commerce VAs who've run stores just like yours — for a fraction of the cost of a local hire or an agency.

This guide covers what a Filipino Shopify VA costs in 2026, exactly what they can run in your store, how to screen for real hands-on skill rather than buzzwords, and how to give them access safely.

Why the Philippines for E-commerce Support?

  • Deep Shopify experience. E-commerce is one of the most common remote specializations in the Philippines. Many VAs have managed multiple stores end to end.
  • Strong English for customer-facing work. Order support, returns, and product descriptions all read naturally to US, UK, and AU shoppers.
  • Tool breadth. Beyond Shopify itself, many VAs know Klaviyo, Gorgias, Oberlo/DSers, Canva, and the popular app ecosystem.
  • Timezone flexibility. Many are willing to cover US or AU hours, which matters for live order support and flash-sale periods.

VA or Developer? Match the Hire to the Work

Most store owners think they need a "Shopify developer" when they actually need a Shopify VA. The distinction saves you money and time.

The Shopify VA

Handles the operational day-to-day: products, orders, content, apps, customer support. This is 90% of what most stores need, ongoing.

The Shopify developer

Handles custom code: Liquid templating, bespoke theme sections, and functionality no app provides. This is project work you need occasionally, not a full-time seat for most brands.

The common, cost-effective setup is a full-time VA for operations plus an occasional developer for projects — rather than overpaying a developer to do routine product uploads.

How to Screen a Filipino Shopify VA

E-commerce is hands-on. Screening should confirm the candidate can actually operate a store, not just describe one.

Step 1: Write a specific job post

Name your platform (Shopify), your niche, the exact tasks (product uploads, order support, Klaviyo, etc.), the apps you use, the hours, and the rate. "Shopify VA for a supplements brand — product management, Gorgias support, and Klaviyo flows, US afternoon hours" attracts far better candidates than "e-commerce VA needed."

Step 2: Give a paid task in a test store

This is the most reliable screen by far. In a development or test store, ask them to upload three products with correct variants, images, SEO fields, and a collection, then install and configure a simple app. Thirty minutes of real work shows you exactly how fluent they are with Shopify's admin — something no resume can fake.

Step 3: Probe for judgment

Ask scenario questions that reveal how they think under pressure:

  • "An order shows paid but didn't create a fulfillment. What do you check?"
  • "Inventory shows 10 in stock but you've had complaints of oversells. Where do you look?"
  • "A customer wants a return outside our policy window. How do you handle it?"

Clear, methodical answers signal someone who has actually run a store, not just watched a course.

Step 4: Check tools and setup

  • Ecosystem depth: Ask which apps and tools they've used and how deeply — Klaviyo, Gorgias, DSers, Judge.me, ReCharge, and similar.
  • Internet: Request a Speedtest screenshot; bulk uploads and live support need a stable connection.
  • Availability: Confirm they can genuinely cover the hours you need, especially for live order support during your customers' day.

Ask for a store they're proud of: A strong e-commerce VA can point to a store they helped run and explain what they owned — product ops, support, or campaigns. Specific, concrete ownership beats a long list of app names every time.

Onboarding and Giving Access Safely

Shopify makes it easy to onboard a VA without handing over the keys to everything. Do it right from day one.

  • Use staff accounts, not your owner login. Create a staff account with only the permissions the role needs — grant products and orders, withhold payouts and account settings until warranted.
  • Enable two-factor authentication on their account, and share app credentials through a password manager rather than in plain text.
  • Document your processes. A short SOP for how you handle uploads, returns, and support prevents inconsistent work and speeds up ramp-up.
  • Review the first two weeks. Check product listings, order handling, and customer replies before granting full autonomy, then step back as the quality holds.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Hiring a developer for VA work. Routine product and order tasks don't need code. Match the hire to the actual work and save money.
  • Skipping the test-store task. A hands-on trial is the only reliable proof of Shopify skill. Never hire on resume alone.
  • Sharing your owner login. Use staff accounts with scoped permissions and 2FA. It's safer and fully reversible.
  • No SOPs. Without documented processes, even a good VA guesses at your standards. Write them down before day one.
  • Vague hours for support. If you need live coverage during a sale or your customers' day, screen for it explicitly.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Filipino Shopify virtual assistant cost?

In 2026, a general Shopify VA handling product uploads, order support, and store admin typically costs $400–$700/month full-time. A VA who also does design, apps, and light theme edits runs $700–$1,100, and a technical Shopify specialist comfortable with Liquid and custom work costs $1,000–$1,600. That's roughly 70–80% less than a US-based equivalent.

What can a Shopify VA actually do in my store?

A capable Shopify VA can add and optimize products, manage inventory and collections, process orders, returns, and refunds, respond to customer inquiries, install and configure apps, make simple theme and content edits, build basic email flows in Klaviyo, and keep your product data and reporting clean. Deep custom development (heavy Liquid or app building) is a specialist skill you should screen for separately.

Do I need a technical Shopify developer or just a VA?

Most stores need a VA, not a developer. Day-to-day e-commerce is product management, order support, content updates, and app configuration — all within a general Shopify VA's range. Hire a developer only for custom theme work, complex Liquid, or building functionality apps don't provide. Many stores keep a full-time VA for operations and bring in a developer occasionally for projects.

Is it safe to give a Shopify VA access to my store?

Yes, using Shopify's staff accounts with granular permissions rather than sharing your owner login. Grant only the sections the role needs (for example products and orders but not payouts or account settings), enable two-factor authentication, and expand access as trust builds. A confidentiality clause in the contract adds further protection. Never share your bank or payment provider credentials.

How do I test a Shopify VA before hiring?

Give a short paid task in a development or test store — for example, upload three products with proper variants, images, SEO titles, and a collection, then install and configure a simple app. This reveals real hands-on Shopify skill far better than a resume. Pair it with questions about how they'd handle a failed order or a sudden inventory issue to gauge judgment.
