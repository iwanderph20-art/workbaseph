How Much Does a Startup Website Cost in 2026? (Full Breakdown)

By WorkBase PH · September 2026 · 8 min read

Search "how much does a startup website cost" and you'll get answers ranging from $0 to $50,000 — and every single one is technically correct. The reason the range is so wide is that the question actually mixes four completely different paths: a DIY builder, a freelancer, an agency, and an in-house team. Each one gives you a different product for a different price.

This breaks down what each path actually costs, what you get for it, and the one line item almost every founder forgets to budget for.

What Actually Drives the Price

Within any of these paths, the quote moves based on the same handful of factors:

  • Page count and complexity. A five-page marketing site is a fraction of the cost of a site with a booking flow, gated content, or an app-like dashboard.
  • Custom design vs. template. A template with your logo dropped in is cheap. A from-scratch brand identity and layout costs more because it's original work, not reuse.
  • Copywriting. Founders often forget this is its own skill and its own line item. Bad copy on a beautifully designed site still won't convert.
  • Ongoing maintenance. Someone has to keep the site updated, secure, and working after launch — that's either your time or a recurring fee.

The Hidden Cost: A Website With No Traffic Plan

Here's where most DIY and single-freelancer budgets go wrong: they price the build and stop there. But a website that nobody finds doesn't do anything for the business, no matter how good it looks. Getting found requires ongoing technical and content SEO, and usually paid ads to fill the gap while organic traffic builds — none of which a one-time build fee covers.

Why this matters: a $2,000 freelance build with zero ongoing SEO work often ends up costing more per lead than a pricier option that bundles growth work in, simply because the cheap site sits empty. Compare total cost per lead, not just the sticker price of the build.

Why Founders End Up Hiring a Team Instead of a Freelancer

The freelancer path looks cheapest on paper, but it usually means managing separate people for design, development, copywriting, and SEO — each with their own timeline, their own invoice, and no single person accountable for whether the site actually performs. Every hand-off is a place where quality (and momentum) can drop.

An in-house team folds all of that under one roof: the same people who built your site also run the SEO and growth work afterward, so there's no gap between "the site is live" and "the site is working." Pricing follows the same logic as everywhere else — website and brand work is a fixed project fee scoped to what you need, and SEO/growth support runs as a monthly retainer once you're live, with ad spend (if you run any) going straight to your own ad account rather than through a markup.

How to Get an Accurate Quote

  1. List what you actually need — page count, any custom features, whether you need copywriting and brand identity or just a build.
  2. Decide if you need growth work too. If lead generation matters, budget for SEO/ads from day one instead of bolting it on later.
  3. Get quotes scoped to that list, not a generic "website package" price, so you're comparing apples to apples.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the cheapest way to get a startup website online?

A DIY builder like Wix, Squarespace, or Carrd is the cheapest path — typically $15–$40/month with no separate build cost, since you design and write it yourself. It's the right call for very early validation, but budget your own time: a founder-built site usually takes 20–40+ hours to look and read well.

How much does a freelancer charge to build a startup website?

A single freelance web designer typically charges $500–$3,000 for a basic marketing site, depending on page count and custom design work. That price usually covers the build only — copywriting, SEO, and ongoing updates are separate freelancers, separate invoices, and separate hand-offs to manage yourself.

How much do web design agencies charge for a startup site?

Traditional agencies typically quote $5,000–$50,000+ for a custom startup website, often with a multi-week discovery phase before design even starts. That price can include copywriting and basic SEO, but ongoing growth work (content, paid ads, reporting) is almost always billed as a separate retainer on top.

Is it worth paying more for a website than a $20/month builder?

It depends on what you need the site to do. If you just need a placeholder while you validate an idea, a builder is fine. Once the site needs to actually generate leads or sales, the build is only half the job — a site nobody finds doesn't convert, which is why cost comparisons that ignore SEO and growth work are comparing the wrong thing.
