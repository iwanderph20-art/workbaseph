Free Filipino VA Contract Template (2026) — Copy & Use

By WorkBase PH · Updated June 2026 · 10 min read

One of the most common questions from first-time employers is: do I need a contract to hire a Filipino virtual assistant? The short answer is yes — even for part-time or informal arrangements. A simple written agreement protects both sides and sets clear expectations from day one.

Below is a straightforward independent contractor agreement you can copy, fill in, and use immediately. It covers the essentials without being overly complicated.

Note: This template is provided for general informational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. For complex arrangements or high-value contracts, consult a qualified attorney familiar with international contractor agreements.

What This Contract Covers

  • Scope of work and deliverables
  • Payment rate and schedule
  • Independent contractor status (not employment)
  • Confidentiality and non-disclosure
  • Intellectual property ownership
  • Termination terms

The Template

Independent Contractor Agreement

This Independent Contractor Agreement ("Agreement") is entered into as of [Date], between:

Client: [Your Full Name or Company Name], located at [Your Address / Country] ("Client")

Contractor: [VA's Full Name], located in the Philippines ("Contractor")

1. Services

The Contractor agrees to provide the following services ("Services") to the Client:

[Describe the specific tasks — e.g., email management, social media scheduling, data entry, customer support, etc.]

Services will be performed on a [full-time / part-time] basis, approximately [X] hours per week, unless otherwise agreed in writing.

2. Payment

The Client agrees to pay the Contractor at a rate of $[Amount] per [hour / week / month], payable via [Wise / PayPal / Payoneer / bank transfer] on a [weekly / bi-weekly / monthly] basis.

Payment shall be made no later than [X] days after the end of each pay period. Late payments beyond this period may incur a late fee of [X]% per month, or as mutually agreed.

3. Independent Contractor Status

The Contractor is an independent contractor, not an employee of the Client. The Contractor is solely responsible for their own taxes, social security contributions, and benefits in the Philippines. Nothing in this Agreement creates an employer-employee relationship, partnership, or joint venture between the parties.

4. Confidentiality

The Contractor agrees to keep all Client information — including but not limited to business operations, client data, financial information, and proprietary processes — strictly confidential during and after the term of this Agreement. The Contractor shall not disclose any such information to third parties without the Client's prior written consent.

5. Intellectual Property

All work product, deliverables, and materials created by the Contractor in the course of performing Services under this Agreement shall be considered work made for hire and shall be the sole property of the Client upon full payment. The Contractor waives any moral rights in such work to the extent permitted by applicable law.

6. Termination

Either party may terminate this Agreement with [14 / 30] days' written notice. The Client may terminate immediately for cause (e.g., breach of confidentiality, non-performance, or misconduct). Upon termination, the Client shall pay the Contractor for all Services rendered up to the termination date.

7. Governing Law

This Agreement shall be governed by the laws of [Client's Country / State]. Any disputes arising under this Agreement shall first be attempted to be resolved through good-faith negotiation between the parties.

8. Entire Agreement

This Agreement constitutes the entire agreement between the parties with respect to the subject matter herein and supersedes all prior discussions, representations, or agreements.

Client Signature:     Date:  

Client Name:  

Contractor Signature:     Date:  

Contractor Name:  

How to Use This Template

  1. Copy the contract text above into a Google Doc or Word document.
  2. Fill in every field marked with [brackets] before sending.
  3. Share with your VA for review — give them a day to read it.
  4. Both parties sign digitally (DocuSign, Adobe Sign, or even a signed PDF scan works).
  5. Each party keeps a copy.

Tip on signing: Free tools like DocuSign or HelloSign allow both parties to sign electronically at no cost for basic use. It's faster and creates a cleaner paper trail than emailing PDFs back and forth.

Key Clauses to Customize

Payment Schedule

Weekly pay is strongly preferred by most Filipino remote workers and builds trust quickly. Bi-weekly is also common. Monthly is generally discouraged — it creates financial stress for contractors and is a leading cause of early turnover.

Notice Period

14 days is reasonable for part-time arrangements. For full-time hires who have left other opportunities to work with you, 30 days is more fair and professional.

Scope of Work

Be as specific as possible in Section 1. Vague scope leads to scope creep, misaligned expectations, and disputes. A clear task list (even a simple bullet list) is better than a general description.

Common Contract Mistakes — and How to Avoid Them

Most contract problems in VA relationships aren't caused by bad intentions — they're caused by unclear language written in a hurry. Here are the most frequent mistakes and how to fix them before you sign.

Mistake 1: Vague scope of work

"Provide administrative support" is not a scope of work. When the scope is vague, both parties fill in the blanks differently — and those different interpretations collide at the worst possible time. Be specific: list the actual tasks, the expected output, and the frequency. If the role evolves, update the agreement in writing (even a simple email thread works as a documented amendment).

Mistake 2: No specified payment date

Saying "paid monthly" is not enough. "Paid on the last Friday of each month via Wise" is. Filipino workers plan their household budgets around their pay schedule. Late or unpredictable payment is the number one reason skilled VAs leave otherwise good working relationships. Specify the date, the method, and what happens if a payment is delayed.

Mistake 3: No intellectual property clause

If your VA creates content, graphics, code, or any work product, and there's no IP clause, ownership can be ambiguous. The template above includes a work-made-for-hire clause that assigns ownership to you upon full payment. Without this, the contractor could technically claim authorship — unlikely but legally relevant for any creative or technical work.

Mistake 4: Too short a notice period for full-time hires

A 7-day notice period on a full-time hire is too short. If your VA has left another job to work with you and builds their income around your engagement, 7 days leaves them financially exposed. 30 days is fair and professional for full-time. 14 days is acceptable for part-time. Shorter notice periods also make it harder to find and train a replacement — which hurts you too.

Mistake 5: No process for disputes

The template includes a good-faith negotiation clause. For most small disputes, this is sufficient — professional Filipino workers rarely escalate disagreements, and most issues resolve with direct conversation. But for higher-value contracts, consider adding an arbitration clause specifying a jurisdiction. Keep it simple: "disputes shall be resolved through binding arbitration in [country]" is usually enough.

Understanding Independent Contractor vs Employee Status

This distinction matters more than most employers realize. Getting it wrong creates legal exposure — even when dealing with a foreign contractor.

Independent contractor characteristics (what you want):

  • Sets their own work schedule (within agreed availability windows)
  • Uses their own equipment and pays for their own tools
  • Can work for multiple clients simultaneously
  • Is paid per project, per hour, or per month — but not entitled to benefits
  • Responsible for their own taxes and social contributions

Employee characteristics (what creates legal complexity):

  • Required to work specific hours dictated by the employer
  • Uses employer-provided equipment exclusively
  • Prohibited from working for other clients
  • Expected to follow detailed company procedures and report to a manager like an internal hire

In practice, most remote VA relationships sit somewhere in the middle. Having an agreed schedule and communication norms is fine — that's normal coordination. What creates risk is treating a contractor like an employee in every meaningful way (exclusive arrangement, employer-owned equipment, mandatory hours) while labelling them a contractor to avoid benefits obligations.

Practical guidance: For standard remote VA arrangements where the worker uses their own equipment, works from their own location, and may have other clients, the independent contractor classification is appropriate and widely accepted. If you're building a large remote team with exclusive full-time workers, consult a Philippine labor attorney about the correct structure.

Handling Contract Renewals and Role Changes

As the working relationship evolves, your contract should too. Here's how to handle common changes cleanly:

Rate increase

A simple email confirming the new rate and effective date is legally sufficient as an amendment to the original contract. Example: "This confirms your rate will increase from $700/month to $800/month effective [date], with all other terms of our agreement remaining the same." Keep a copy.

Adding new responsibilities

If you're adding significant new tasks to the role, update Section 1 (Scope of Work) in writing. If the new tasks are substantially different from the original role, a new rate negotiation is fair and expected.

Moving from part-time to full-time

Treat this as a new agreement. Issue an updated contract with the revised hours, rate, and any updated terms (notice period, for example, should increase if you're moving to full-time).

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a lawyer to use this contract template?

For standard VA arrangements — part-time or full-time, with a single remote worker for general business support — this template is designed to be used without a lawyer. It covers the core protections both parties need. For complex arrangements (managing sensitive financial data, building proprietary software, or contracts worth $50,000+ per year), a review by a qualified attorney is worth the cost.

Does Philippine law govern contracts between a Filipino worker and a foreign employer?

Generally no — you specify the governing law in the contract (Section 7 in the template). Most foreign employers specify their own country's laws. Philippine labor law applies to employees of Philippine-registered businesses, not to independent contractors working for foreign employers under foreign-law contracts. That said, this is a simplified overview — consult a legal professional for your specific situation.

Does the contract need to be notarized?

No. For independent contractor agreements between private parties, notarization is not required for the contract to be valid and enforceable. Electronic signatures (via DocuSign, Adobe Sign, or even a clear email confirmation of terms) are legally recognized in most jurisdictions. Keep a signed copy on file.

What if the VA refuses to sign a contract?

A professional, experienced Filipino VA will have no objection to signing a contract — it protects them as much as it protects you. Reluctance to sign may indicate they're working under a competing non-compete clause, have something to hide, or aren't serious about the role. Don't proceed without a signed agreement.

Can I use the same contract template for all roles — VA, bookkeeper, designer, developer?

Yes, the template works across all independent contractor roles. The key customizations are Section 1 (Scope of Work — make it role-specific) and the payment terms. For technical roles involving significant IP creation (software development, branding), make sure the IP clause in Section 5 is clearly worded. For roles involving sensitive financial data access, you may want to strengthen the confidentiality clause.

What happens to the contract if the VA moves to a different country?

The contract remains in effect as written. The worker's location doesn't affect the validity of the agreement or your obligations as a client. Practically, payment methods may need to change, and you should confirm that their new location still complies with your data handling requirements (particularly if you operate in the EU and are subject to GDPR).
