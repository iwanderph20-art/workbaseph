Hire a Filipino VA With No Bidding Fees (2026): Why Flat-Rate Beats Commission Platforms

By WorkBase PH · Updated July 2026 · 8 min read

Search for "hire a Filipino virtual assistant" and every platform promises talent. What they don't advertise on the homepage is how they get paid — and that's where most of your real cost hides. Bidding fees, connects, percentage commissions, and monthly minimums quietly add up to far more than the hourly rate you agreed with your VA.

This guide breaks down the three fee models you'll run into, shows the true cost of each over a year, and explains why a flat-fee, no-commission model is usually the cheapest way to hire a verified Filipino VA for any ongoing role.

What "No Bidding Fees" Actually Means

On bidding marketplaces, two separate charges eat into a hire:

  • Bid fees on the worker's side. Freelancers spend "connects" or credits just to submit a proposal — so the best candidates ration their applications, and some skip your job entirely.
  • Commission on every payment. The platform takes a percentage of everything the worker earns, for the entire life of the contract. That cost is baked into the rate the worker quotes you, whether you see it on the invoice or not.

"No bidding fees" means neither of those exists. Freelancers apply for free, keep 100% of what you pay, and you're not charged per message or per proposal to reach them. On WorkBase PH, employers pay a single flat fee to post a role and get every applicant's full profile and contact details from day one — no paywall, no per-bid charge, no percentage skim.

Why this matters for candidate quality: Experienced Filipino specialists know exactly how much commission platforms take from them. A no-commission model consistently attracts more professional, career-minded applicants — because they keep everything they earn.

When a Bidding Platform Might Still Make Sense

To be fair, no single model wins every time:

  • A true one-off micro-task. If you need two hours of work once and never again, a bidding platform's commission is small in absolute terms and the marketplace escrow can be convenient.
  • You want global, not Philippines-focused, talent. Broad marketplaces span every country. If you specifically want verified Filipino specialists, a PH-focused platform gives you a more relevant pool with less filtering.

For anything ongoing — a VA, an executive assistant, a bookkeeper, a support rep you'll work with for months — the flat-fee, no-commission model is almost always the cheaper and cleaner choice.

How to Hire a Filipino VA With Zero Bidding Fees

Frequently Asked Questions

What does "no bidding fees" actually mean?

On bidding platforms like Upwork or Freelancer, workers spend credits or "connects" just to submit a proposal, and the platform takes a percentage (often 5–20%) of everything they earn. "No bidding fees" means neither of those exists: freelancers apply for free and keep 100% of what you pay them, and you don't pay a per-message or per-bid charge to reach candidates. On WorkBase PH, employers pay a single flat fee to post a job and freelancers pay nothing, ever.

How much does it cost to hire a Filipino VA with no commission?

On a flat-rate platform, your only platform cost is the posting fee. On WorkBase PH that is a one-time $18 flat fee for a job post, with full access to every applicant's profile and contact details from day one. Why do commission platforms cost more over time?

A commission is charged on every payment for as long as the working relationship lasts. A 10% cut on a VA earning $1,200/month is $120 every month, or $1,440 per year, on top of what you actually pay the worker. A flat-fee platform charges once at the point of hire and never touches the ongoing relationship, so the longer you work with someone, the larger the savings compared to a percentage model.

Do Filipino freelancers pay any fees on WorkBase PH?

No. Freelancers and specialists never pay to create a profile, apply to jobs, or receive payment. WorkBase PH takes no commission from workers, which means the people you hire keep 100% of their earnings. This tends to attract more experienced, professionally-minded candidates who won't work on platforms that skim their pay.

Is a flat fee always cheaper than a bidding or commission platform?

For most employers, yes — especially for ongoing roles. Flat-rate wins clearly when you hire someone for more than a few weeks, because commission keeps charging month after month while a flat fee is paid once. Bidding platforms can look cheaper up front for a tiny one-off task, but their percentage cut and worker-side fees usually make them more expensive for any real, ongoing hire.
