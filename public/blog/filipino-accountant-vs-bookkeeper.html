Filipino Accountant vs. Bookkeeper: Which Do You Need?

By WorkBase PH · Updated September 2026 · 7 min read

"Bookkeeper" and "accountant" get used interchangeably in a lot of job posts, and it leads to mismatched hires — an employer expecting tax strategy from someone hired to reconcile receipts, or paying accountant rates for basic data entry. The two roles are genuinely different in scope, and knowing which one you need before you hire saves both money and frustration.

The Core Difference

BookkeeperAccountant
Main jobRecords and organizes transactionsInterprets records, prepares statements and filings
Typical tasksCategorizing expenses, bank reconciliation, invoicing, payroll data entryFinancial statements, tax preparation, financial analysis, advisory
FrequencyOngoing, often daily or weeklyPeriodic — monthly, quarterly, or annual
Best forKeeping books current and accurateUnderstanding what the numbers mean and staying compliant

Think of it this way: a bookkeeper builds and maintains the dataset. An accountant reads that dataset and tells you what it means, what you owe, and what to do next.

Which One Do You Actually Need?

Hire a bookkeeper if:

  • Your books are behind or messy and need to be brought current
  • You need someone to handle invoicing, expense categorization, and reconciliation on an ongoing basis
  • You mainly need accurate, organized data — not analysis or tax strategy

Hire an accountant if:

  • You need financial statements prepared for lenders, investors, or your own decision-making
  • You need help with tax planning, filings, or compliance
  • You want strategic input on pricing, margins, cash flow, or financial structure

Most growing businesses eventually need both: a bookkeeper keeping transactions current day-to-day, and an accountant reviewing the books periodically for statements, tax prep, and higher-level guidance. Many businesses start with just a bookkeeper and add accounting support as complexity grows.

How to Screen Either Role

For a bookkeeper

  • Confirm hands-on experience with your specific software (QuickBooks Online is standard for US clients, Xero for AU/UK).
  • Give a short paid test: reconcile a sample bank statement or categorize a batch of sample transactions.
  • Ask how they handle a discrepancy they can't immediately explain — a good bookkeeper flags it rather than guessing or ignoring it.

For an accountant

  • Ask about relevant credentials (a CPA-equivalent qualification in the Philippines, or specific experience with your country's tax and reporting requirements).
  • Request a sample financial statement or report they've prepared (with client details redacted).
  • Clarify upfront what they can and can't do for your specific jurisdiction — most can prepare and organize data, but final tax filing and sign-off in your home country usually still requires a locally licensed professional.

Not legal or tax advice: requirements for who can legally file taxes or sign off on financial statements vary by country. Confirm your specific compliance requirements with a licensed professional in your own jurisdiction before finalizing how you structure this hire.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Hiring a bookkeeper and expecting tax strategy. That's an accountant's job — a bookkeeper's role is data accuracy, not advisory.
  • Paying accountant rates for data entry. If the actual work is transaction categorization and reconciliation, you're overpaying for a bookkeeper-level task.
  • Assuming any Filipino accountant can sign off on your home-country tax filing. Confirm your specific jurisdiction's requirements before relying on this for compliance.
  • Letting books fall behind before hiring. A backlog is more expensive to clean up than staying current from the start.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a bookkeeper and an accountant?

A bookkeeper records day-to-day financial transactions — categorizing expenses, reconciling bank accounts, sending invoices, and keeping your books current. An accountant interprets those books to prepare financial statements, tax filings, and higher-level financial guidance. Bookkeeping is data entry and organization; accounting is analysis and compliance.

Do I need a bookkeeper, an accountant, or both?

Most small businesses start with a bookkeeper to keep transactions current and accurate. As revenue, tax complexity, or investor/lender reporting requirements grow, an accountant becomes necessary for tax filings, financial statement preparation, and strategic advice. Many businesses eventually use both, with the bookkeeper feeding clean data to the accountant.

How much does a Filipino accountant cost compared to a bookkeeper?

In 2026, Filipino bookkeepers typically run $400-$1,400/month depending on experience and transaction volume. Filipino accountants, who handle higher-level financial statement preparation and tax work, typically run $700-$2,200/month depending on qualifications and scope.

Can a Filipino accountant file my taxes?

A Filipino accountant can prepare and organize the financial data your tax filing requires, but tax law is jurisdiction-specific. For your home country's actual tax filing and signature, you generally still need a locally licensed tax preparer or CPA — many businesses use a Filipino accountant for preparation and a local professional for final filing and sign-off.

What software should a Filipino accountant or bookkeeper know?

QuickBooks Online is the most common for US-based clients, and Xero is standard for Australian and UK clients. Ask specifically about experience with your accounting software and your industry, since retail, e-commerce, and service businesses have different bookkeeping patterns.
