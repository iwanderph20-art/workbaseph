How to Pay a Filipino Virtual Assistant (2026): Wise vs Payoneer vs Bank Transfer

By WorkBase PH · Updated July 2026 · 9 min read

Hiring a Filipino virtual assistant is the easy part. The question employers ask right after they've made an offer is almost always the same: how do I actually pay them? You want a method that's low-fee, arrives reliably, and doesn't create a monthly headache for either side.

The good news is that paying a VA in the Philippines in 2026 is straightforward. Filipino remote workers are used to receiving international payments, and the infrastructure is mature. This guide compares the four methods people actually use — Wise, Payoneer, PayPal, and direct bank transfer — on the things that matter: fees, speed, exchange rates, and how much work each one is to run.

Worth knowing up front: On WorkBase PH you agree on a rate directly with your hire and pay them directly. We take no cut of wages and don't sit between you and your VA on payday — so you're free to choose whichever method below works best for both of you.

For most employer-to-VA relationships, the practical choice comes down to Wise or Payoneer. The other two have real drawbacks that add up quickly at a monthly cadence. Let's go through each.

Wise — The Default Choice for Most Employers

Wise is the most popular way to pay Filipino remote workers, and for good reason: it uses the real mid-market exchange rate (the one you see on Google) and charges a small, transparent conversion fee on top — usually well under 1% for USD-to-PHP. There's no hidden markup baked into the exchange rate, which is where most other services quietly make their money.

How it works

  • You create a free Wise Business account (individual accounts work too, but Business gives you invoicing and batch payments).
  • Your VA gives you their Philippine bank account details, or their Wise account email.
  • You send the payment in USD (or your local currency); Wise converts to PHP and deposits into their local bank — usually the same day for peso transfers.

Why VAs like it: the money lands directly in their local peso bank account (BDO, BPI, GCash, and most Philippine banks are supported), so there's no extra withdrawal step or ATM run. What you send is very close to what they receive.

Cost example

On a $500 monthly payment, Wise typically costs $3–$5 total, and your VA receives the near-exact peso equivalent at the true exchange rate. Over a year that's roughly $40–$60 in fees — compared with $200+ on PayPal for the same volume.

Payoneer — Common, but Watch the Fees

Payoneer is widely used across the Filipino freelance community, so there's a good chance your VA already has an account. Its main advantage is familiarity and the option for the VA to hold a USD balance and withdraw to their local bank when the rate is favorable.

The trade-off is cost. Payoneer's fees are higher than Wise — typically around 2% on card-funded payments, plus a currency-conversion markup when the VA withdraws to pesos. If your VA specifically requests Payoneer because it's what they already use, it's perfectly fine. But if you're choosing fresh, Wise usually leaves more money in your VA's pocket for the same outlay.

Payoneer makes the most sense when:

  • Your VA already has an established Payoneer account and prefers it.
  • You're paying multiple contractors and want one consistent platform.
  • Your VA wants to hold USD rather than convert to pesos immediately.

PayPal — Convenient, but Expensive

PayPal is the method most first-time employers reach for because it's familiar. It's fast and easy to set up, but it's the most expensive option on a recurring basis. Between the cross-border fee (around 4–5%) and an unfavorable built-in exchange rate, a $500 payment can lose $25–$40 to fees — and your VA absorbs the poor conversion when they withdraw to pesos.

Rule of thumb: PayPal is fine for a small one-off payment (a paid trial task, a small milestone) where convenience beats cost. For ongoing monthly wages, the fees compound into real money — switch to Wise once the relationship is established.

Direct Bank / SWIFT Transfer — Usually Not Worth It

A traditional international wire from your bank to your VA's Philippine bank works, but it's the least efficient option for typical VA amounts. Banks charge a flat fee ($15–$50), add their own exchange-rate markup, and sometimes intermediary banks skim additional fees along the way — so the amount that actually arrives can be unpredictable. It only starts to make sense for large, infrequent fixed payments, and even then Wise usually wins on total cost.

How to Set Up a Payment Schedule That Works

The method is only half the equation. A clear, predictable payment routine builds trust and keeps a good VA loyal. Here's a simple structure that works for most employers:

1. Agree on the cadence in writing

Most VA relationships run on one of three cadences: weekly, bi-weekly, or monthly. Bi-weekly (every two weeks) is a popular middle ground — frequent enough to keep cash flow comfortable for the VA, infrequent enough to keep admin light for you. Whatever you choose, put it in your agreement along with the exact pay dates.

2. Decide hourly vs fixed monthly

For part-time or variable work, hourly with a simple time log (Toggl, Clockify, or Hubstaff) is transparent for both sides. For full-time VAs, a fixed monthly salary is cleaner and feels more like employment, which helps retention. Fixed also means predictable costs for you and predictable income for them.

3. Handle the first payment carefully

For a brand-new hire you don't yet have a track record with, a short paid trial (the first week, or a small defined project) paid promptly on completion establishes trust in both directions. Pay it exactly when you said you would — on-time first payment is the single strongest signal that you're a good employer to work for.

4. Send a simple payslip or confirmation

A one-line message — "Sent $500 via Wise for July, reference #12345" — with the payment takes seconds and removes any ambiguity. Filipino VAs often keep informal records for their own budgeting, and a consistent confirmation habit marks you as an organized, reliable client.

13th month pay: In the Philippines, a "13th month" bonus (roughly one extra month's pay, typically paid in December) is a deeply ingrained norm for employees. VAs are independent contractors, so it isn't legally required — but many employers who want to retain a great long-term VA choose to offer an annual bonus in a similar spirit. It's a small gesture that carries real cultural weight.

Do I Need to Worry About Taxes or Compliance?

When you hire a Filipino VA as an independent contractor, they're responsible for their own taxes in the Philippines — you're not their employer of record and you don't withhold Philippine taxes. On your side, payments to an overseas contractor may be a deductible business expense; keep clean records of each payment (amount, date, method, and an invoice from your VA) for your own bookkeeping and tax filing.

If you're in the US, contractors outside the US who perform their work outside the US generally do not require 1099 filing, but you may want to collect a Form W-8BEN for your records. Rules vary by country, so confirm the specifics with your accountant — but for the vast majority of employers, paying an overseas VA is simply a matter of recording the expense.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the cheapest way to pay a Filipino virtual assistant?

Wise is the cheapest reliable option for most employers. It uses the real mid-market exchange rate and charges a small, transparent conversion fee — usually under 1% for USD-to-PHP — with the money landing directly in the VA's local peso bank account. For recurring monthly wages, it consistently costs a fraction of PayPal.

Should I pay my VA in USD or Philippine pesos?

Either works, but paying so the money arrives as pesos in their local bank is usually most convenient for the VA and avoids a second conversion step. With Wise, you send in your currency and they receive pesos automatically. Some VAs prefer to hold USD (via Payoneer) and convert when the rate suits them — ask your VA which they prefer.

How often should I pay a Filipino VA?

Weekly, bi-weekly, or monthly are all common. Bi-weekly is a popular balance between the VA's cash flow and your admin load. The most important thing isn't the frequency — it's consistency. Pick a schedule, put it in writing, and always pay on time.

Is it safe to send money to a VA I just hired?

For a first payment with someone you don't yet have a track record with, start with a short paid trial or a small first milestone rather than a full month upfront. Once you've confirmed the quality of their work and their reliability, move to your regular schedule. Hiring through a platform where most candidates have a video or audio intro reduces the risk considerably compared with an anonymous marketplace.

Do Filipino VAs expect a 13th month bonus?

As independent contractors they're not legally entitled to it, so it isn't expected the way it is for local employees. That said, offering an annual bonus — especially around December — is a well-received gesture that helps retain a great VA long-term, because the 13th month is a strong cultural norm in the Philippines.
